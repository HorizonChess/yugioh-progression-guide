# Pack Opening Rules and Ratios

## About Our Pack Puller Ratios

### What Makes Our Pack Simulator Unique
We've spent a lot of time scouring the internet for information regarding the exact rarity ratios associated with each set.
This is a somewhat arduous task- because ratios aren't always printed on packs, we have to rely on peoples' experiences relayed via forums years ago, or watching pack pulling videos and extrapolating out the odds from those.
We have done our best to make our pack puller consistent with the actual ratios used for each physical set, and those ratios are detailed below.
A huge thanks goes out to Ruxin34- some of the most complex and unknown pack ratios came from one of us sitting down to watch set unboxings on his channel.

### Hobby Box vs Retail Box
In physical releases, hobby boxes and retail boxes have different cards considered higher rarities from each other.
In early sets they had differing secret rares, but the biggest difference between the two was during the era of ultimate rares.
Retail boxes only had ultimate rares which were higher-rarity versions of rares, but hobby boxes only had ultimate rares of supers or ultras.
Because we are not creating and distributing boxes, though we maintain the ultra rare ratios per pack, we are using a mix of hobby and retail boxes for our packs.
This means that ultimate rares can be higher-rarity versions of rares, ultras, or supers, and you can pull any secret rares from both box types for a set.

### First Edition Prints
In first edition printings of the earliest sets there was not guaranteed to be a rare in every pack. However, reprints of older sets did guarantee a rare in every pack.
With this in mind we ignore first edition prints, therefore none of our cards or packs will make a distinction between first edition and other editions. In those early sets you will be guaranteed a rare in every pack.

### How We Use Ratios to Generate Pack Pulls
Using LOB as an example, we take the rarity ratios for each rarity and construct a "hat" out of 372 possibilites for the foil (LCM of each ratio).
For each possible rarity we divide the hat size by the ratio and fill in the extra space with commons (or whatever the card would be if it isn't in the ratio).
So LOB has 12 secret rares, 31 ultra rares, 62 super rares, and 267 commons in this hat.
Then we just pick a rarity from the hat and that is the rarity for the foil in the pack.

### How We Handle Short Prints
We do not have ratios for Short Printed cards, unfortunately there is not enough data to be able to figure out what ratio short printed cards are pulled at.
For this reason we ignore short printed cards as a use case, and just consider them the rarity they are as a card.

### Box Odds
Unlike loose packs, packs in a box have a "guaranteed" distribution of rarity pulls based on the ratios within that box.
For example, the in a 24-Pack LOB Box - which has a 1:6 Super Rare Chance, a 1:12 Ultra Rare Chance, and a 1:32 Secret Rare Chance:
The Box would have 4 Super Rares, 2 Ultra Rares, and 1 or 0 Secret Rares.

### Reporting Incorrect Ratios
The ratios for our pack simulator are the one of our biggest reasons for making this tool, and making sure they are as accurate as possible is the goal.
If you believe we have our ratios wrong for any pack or era please reach out to us via our email admin@ygoprog.com with sources!
The sources part is extremely important here, a large amount of our data on the earlier eras of yugioh involve debates about pack ratios on forums and extrapolation of ratios from watching bulk set openings.

## Pack Ratios Per Set

### Core Sets
Every core set is made up of 9 cards per pack. There are 7 guaranteed commons, 1 guaranteed rare (up until Eternity Code where they discontinued rares), and the ninth card is either a foil pulled via the ratios, or a common (until Breakers of Shadow, from which point the foil became guaranteed).
Most core sets can be separated into eras where the ratios stayed the same for several sets, and we have grouped them as such.

#### Pre Soul of the Duelist
For every set before Soul of the Duelist they used these ratios for the ninth card.
- Super 1:6
- Ultra 1:12
- Secret 1:31

#### Soul of the Duelist through Cyberdark Impact
These sets are unique because the chance for an ultimate rare is actually two times the chance for an ultra (Ruxin talks about it briefly in this video)
We couldn't find any good corroborating sources online for these ratios so we had to watch unboxing videos to verify the pack odds for the ninth card.
- Super 1:6
- Ultra 1:24
- Ultimate 1:12

#### Strike of Neos and Force of the Breaker
These two sets used the exact same ratios as the previous sets, except they also added back secret rares.
- Super 1:6
- Ultra 1:24
- Secret 1:31
- Ultimate 1:12

#### Tactical Evolution through Absolute Powerforce
These sets are the first sets to include ghost rares. The ratio for these is talked about in this blog post which talks about discontinuing ghost rares for Breakers of Shadows. As part of this post they talk about what the historic ratio was for these sets.
- Super 1:5
- Ultra 1:12
- Secret 1:31
- Ultimate 1:31
- Ghost 1:288

#### Shining Darkness through Dimension of Chaos
In these sets the likelihood of getting a secret or ultra was improved to be 1 or 2 per box.
- Super 1:5
- Ultra 1:12
- Secret 1:23
- Ultimate 1:23
- Ghost 1:288

#### Breakers of Shadow through Dark Neostorm
These sets greatly altered pack composition. Instead of the ninth card being either a common or a foil, the ninth card was guaranteed to be foil (super, ultra, or secret). They also removed ghost rares from this point onwards. Source
- Ultra 1:6
- Secret 1:12
- If it isn't an ultra or secret it is a super

#### Rising Rampage through Ignition Assault
These sets added a new rarity type of starlight rare, which could contend for the foil slot.
- Ultra 1:6
- Secret 1:12
- Starlight 1:576

#### Eternity Code to Present
These sets use the same ratios as the rising rampage era, but from this point on rares had been discontinued from being a part of packs. This means that packs consist of 8 commons, and a foil, with the foil being the same ratio as above.
- Ultra 1:6
- Secret 1:12
- Starlight 1:576


### Tournament Pack Rewards
This category consists of Tournament Packs, Champion Packs, Turbo Packs, Astral Packs, and OTS Packs.
These packs are all 3 cards per pack, with two guaranteed commons, and a third card with different ratios.

#### Tournament Packs
For tournament packs you could get a common for the third card.
- Rare 1:3
- Super 1:18
- Ultra 1:108

#### Champion Packs
For these packs onwards you could no longer get a common as the third card. If it was not one of the possible foils it would be a rare.
- Super 1:12
- Ultra 1:48

#### Turbo Packs
- Super 1:12
- Ultra 1:32
- Ultimate 1:32

#### Astral and OTS Packs
For these two pack types we couldn't find any good sources for rarity ratios, so we referred to Ruxin34 pack openings to find the approximate ratio for ultimates.
Also for these packs are are no rares, if the third card is not an ultimate it will be a super.
- Ultimate 1:20


### Duelist Packs
Duelist packs have 5 cards per pack, 3 commons, 1 rare, and the last card being common if it's not a foil.

#### Duelist Pack Jaden Yuki 1 until Duelist Pack Jesse Anderson
- Super 1:6
- Ultra 1:24

#### Duelist Pack Yusei until Duelist Pack Yugi
- Super 1:5
- Ultra 1:15

#### Duelist Pack Kaiba
This is the only duelist pack we found which has ultimate rares, and has its ratios listed on the official product list
- Super 1:5
- Ultra 1:15
- Ultimate 1:24

#### Remaining Duelist Packs
Stopped printing ultimates and went back to the previous ratios
- Super 1:5
- Ultra 1:15


### Reprint Packs
Most Reprint packs cover all Dark Beginning, Dark Revelation, and Dark Legends packs. These packs have 12 cards per pack, and will always have 6 monsters, 3 traps, and 3 spells.
The packs also always had 10 commons, 1 rare, and the 12th card was either a common or a foil.
There are also retro packs which have 9 cards a pack, 7 commons, 1 rare, and the 9th card was either foil or common.

#### Dark Beginning 1 until Dark Revelation 3
- Super 1:6
- Ultra 1:12

#### Dark Revelation 4
For both Dark Revelation 4 and Dark Legends it was difficult to find a secret rare ratio for the packs, so we defaulted to using the secret rare ratio at the time which was 1:31 (Tactical Evolution Era).
- Super 1:4
- Ultra 1:8
- Secret 1:31

#### Dark Legends
Dark Legends has no pull rate for secret rares, Gorz is a promo card that came with the packs. As a result it is not a card you can pull from our pack opener.
- Super 1:5
- Ultra 1:12

#### Retro Packs 1 and 2
For these packs we could not find good information on the secret rare ratio, so we used the ratio from core sets at their time.
This feels correct as well, because the super and ultra ratios mirrored the core sets at the time.
- Super 1:5
- Ultra 1:12
- Secret 1:31


### Premium Gold Packs
Premium gold packs have 5 cards per pack, and have different buckets for the gold rare. You are guaranteed to get one gold rare from a certain number range of cards, a couple from another, and then the gold secrets.

- 2 Gold Secrets
